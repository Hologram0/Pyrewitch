This place was much more drafty than Eden was used to. At this time of night, they'd normally be lying in a bed that was perfectly arranged and perfectly comfortable. It was a perfect bed for perfect sleep. At least, that's what their house keeper would always say. The bed Eden lay in tonight was none of those things. The padding wasn't quite as soft, the blankets were well worn, and the house creaked. 'The house creaking is not the fault of the bed' Eden thought 'and, perfect bed or not, it seems such a perfect sleep seems to evade me until the early hours of the morning' . But without his books to keep them company, Eden simply grew bored.

He jolted awake to the sound of a dog barking, which stopped as soon as it began. Certain that if there was a dog, someone would have introduced them, Eden sat up and listened intently, trying to see how far away the animal was. 'Surely just a neighbor's pet.' he reasoned, and then they heard it again. Close. Inside the house? They got out of their bed and as quietly as possible walked towards the door, pausing with their hand over the doorknob. They could feel his heart pounding in his chest. Another bark, as though the dog was angry at something. Eden felt sick. They glanced around the room for the heaviest thing he could find. Not much, but maybe the lamp could work in a pinch. He took a breath, then turned the doorknob, wincing at the creaking of the door. As soon as he could fit through, he dashed across the hall to the fighter lady's room.

Before he could knock, he froze as a familiar light filled the darkened hall. The pounding of his heart was so loud in their ears that they were certain it could wake up everyone in the house. Out of luck, the only sound remained their heartbeat and their breath, both seemed deafeningly loud, and both brought more and more pain as moments passed.

The whimper of a dog snapped them out of their panic, as they turned to face the ghost of a once majestic creature.

Eden couldn't quite remember what people called this kind of dog but they were certain that, in life, this one would fit in with a flock of sheep. That was assuming the dog was at half this size in life. In death, it seemed to be taller than Eden. It was sitting in front of a window just down the staircase, whining at something outside.

Now conscious of waking up the house, and gaining confidence that they were not in danger of an attack by a feral animal, Eden made his way quietly down the steps, approaching the dog. The dogs ear twitched at the sound of a particularly squeaky bottom step, then glanced back at Eden. Eden froze as the dog stood and walked over to the staircase, panting and tail wagging.

They could see their hand shake as he reached out to pet the animal, and exhaled when they realized the dog was delighted to have a friend. They could feel the dogs thoughts the same as any ghost he met, and this ghost was here to protect people. The dog glanced out the window again and Eden could feel the growl growing in its throat.

'Show me' Eden whispered, taking the final step to the first floor. The dog immediately turned and bounded to the window, Eden following quickly behind.

At first, there was nothing to see. No people, no ghosts, just the moon, and the darkened street. As Eden focused, they could see a red glow start to fill in the edges of their vision. He focused on the glow, and allowed his vision to slip past the normal world and into the alternative world, as Eden called it.

A feather lay on the windowsill. An evil thing if Eden had ever seen one. While he couldn't see where it would have come from he could see that it needed to be removed. Something about the sickly red of the feather was seeping into the wood of the house in this alternative place.

Eden traced their fingers around the frame of the windowsill, and found the latch. They pushed the window open and reached for the feather. The dog barked, and he flinched.

The ghost picked up the feather in its teeth, and jumped through the window, running off without so much as a glance backwards. Eden stood at the open window for a while. Eventually they closed the window and sat down on the stairs, waiting to see if the dog would come back before dawn. It did not.
